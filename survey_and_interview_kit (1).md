# Duel Mind AI: Follow-up Research Kit

Goal: collect real answers in 24 hours to back the Blind Spots hypothesis and shape the AI feature. Report only what you actually collect, with the real sample size.

## Part 1: Follow-up survey (Google Form, about 3 minutes)

Intro text for the form: "We are students at IIM Visakhapatnam building a tool that helps founders find hidden risks in their startup ideas. This takes 3 minutes. Responses are anonymous and used only for a class project."

1. Which best describes you?
   - Student with a startup idea
   - Working professional with a startup idea
   - Founder or early-stage team member
   - Mentor, investor or evaluator
   - Other

2. In the last 12 months, have you pitched or reviewed a business idea? (Yes / No)

3. How honest and useful was the feedback you got on your last idea? (1 = polite but useless, 5 = brutally honest and useful)

4. Which parts of a business idea are hardest for you to stress test? (Select up to 3)
   - Unit economics (costs, margins, CAC)
   - Customer demand
   - Execution and operations
   - Competition
   - Regulation or legal
   - Team capability

5. How do you check your idea for hidden risks today? (Select all that apply)
   - Ask friends or classmates
   - Ask a mentor or professor
   - Use ChatGPT, Gemini or similar
   - Build my own spreadsheet
   - I do not check formally

6. If a tool reviewed your idea, which output would help you most? (Pick one)
   - A ranked list of risks with what to test next
   - A full investment memo
   - A score out of 10
   - A transcript of an AI debate

7. Which details would you be comfortable typing into an AI tool? (Select all that apply)
   - Problem and target customer
   - Pricing and revenue model
   - Cost and margin numbers
   - Full financials
   - Team names
   - None of these

8. How concerned would you be about entering your idea into an AI tool? (1 = not at all, 5 = very concerned)

9. Open answer: What would make you trust, or not trust, a critique written by AI?

Why these questions: Q3 to Q5 test the Blind Spots hypothesis. Q6 decides the output format. Q7 decides which input fields the form asks for. Q8 and Q9 feed the privacy and ethics slide.

## Part 2: Short interviews (10 minutes each, aim for 2 or 3)

Who: classmates, friends or juniors who have pitched or are building an idea. Ask for permission, keep them anonymous ("Interviewee A") and note the date.

1. Tell me about the last time you got feedback on a business idea. Who gave it and what happened next?
2. What risk did you miss, or find too late?
3. How do you currently check your numbers, such as pricing, costs and margins?
4. Have you tried ChatGPT or Gemini for this? What worked and what annoyed you?
5. (Show the sample risk list from the app.) What is useful here? What is missing? Would you act on it?
6. What worries you about putting your idea into a tool like this?

Write down direct quotes, as the brief asks for evidence from interviews.

## Part 3: Test script for the deployed app (3 people, 10 minutes each)

Ask each person to open the live link and do this without help while you watch:
1. Fill the form with their own idea, or load the sample pitch.
2. Run the debate.
3. Tell you the top risk in their own words.
4. Say what they would do next.

Record: did they get stuck anywhere, how long the debate took, and one thing they would change. These notes become the "debug and iterate" evidence in the deck.

## How to report the results

- State the real sample size, for example "n = 12".
- If the sample is small, say "directional" and do not present percentages as conclusive.
- Keep Phase 1 figures labeled as Phase 1 and the new figures labeled as Phase 2.
